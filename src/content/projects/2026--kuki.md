Disinformation spreads across platforms and languages. In Germany, many people in Russian- and Turkish-speaking communities follow content in Russian or Turkish alongside German-language media. This multilingualism broadens the range of opinions, but it is also exploited by actors who deliberately spread manipulative narratives in these information spaces to drive social polarization. Existing AI systems for detecting disinformation reach their limits here, as they capture cultural and linguistic nuances only poorly. kuKI researches and develops a culturally sensitive monitoring and analysis infrastructure for disinformation in German, Turkish and Russian. The project combines AI-based language analysis with editorial and civil-society expertise to systematically identify and analyze manipulative content and make it visible through fact-checks and participatory formats. At its core, the existing Faktenforum is extended into a trilingual platform that its community uses and shapes together. A language model tailored to multilingual audiences and a disinformation knowledge graph that connects campaigns, sources and channels of distribution complement the platform. Partners from FZI Forschungszentrum Informatik, Trier University, CORRECTIV, CeMAS and Goethe University Frankfurt work together in the project.
