More and more people learn about science informally, through social media, online forums and, increasingly, conversations with AI chatbots. In these settings, scientific findings are often simplified, exaggerated or distorted. Emotions play a key role: content that triggers fear, anger or excitement tends to spread further and can make distorted claims seem more convincing. How emotions and distorted science interact, and how AI systems affect this, is still poorly understood. EMO-SCI is a Franco-German project that studies these interactions in online science discourse. Partners from Heinrich Heine University Düsseldorf, Trier University, the i3S laboratory and the coordinating lab LIRMM are building new datasets, models and tools. These will be used to analyse how emotions drive the spread of scientific misinformation and disinformation, including in exchanges with AI chatbots. The goal is to better understand these effects and to provide tools that help counter scientific disinformation online.
