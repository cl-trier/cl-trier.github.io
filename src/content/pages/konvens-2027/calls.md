# Call for Workshop, Tutorial, and Shared Task Proposals

KONVENS 2027 invites proposals for **workshops**, **tutorials**, and **shared tasks** to be held at Trier University on Sep. 06 (Mon) and Sep. 07 (Tue), 2027, ahead of the main conference. We welcome proposals from all areas of computational linguistics and natural language processing. Proposals that connect to this year's theme, *Linguistic Diversity in a World of Synthetic Text*, are particularly encouraged.

## Linguistic Diversity in a World of Synthetic Text

Language models now produce synthetic text at unprecedented scale, and this text flows back into the corpora, platforms, and research instruments our field relies on. We are looking for satellite events that help the community describe, measure, and reflect on this development. Possible directions include, but are not limited to:

- **Attribution and explainability:** explainable, linguistically grounded measurements for attributing text to human or machine authors, and their robustness across unseen domains, generators, and adversarial rewrites.
- **Linguistic profiles of synthetic text:** lexical, morphosyntactic, semantic, and pragmatic patterns that set generated text apart from human language, including register, genre, and stylistic variation.
- **Ecological validity:** the gap between fluent, normative model output and the language people actually use, including informal, non-standard, and noisy discourse.
- **Language models as research instruments:** validating models used as annotators, simulated social media users, or survey respondents against authentic human data.
- **Homogenization and feedback loops:** how dialects, sociolects, minority perspectives, and smaller language communities are compressed into a homogeneous default, and how recursive training on model output affects lexical, syntactic, and semantic diversity.
- **Variation and low-resource settings:** orthographic and lexical variation in small and semi-standardized languages, dedicated resources and models for low-resource languages, and socially loaded discourse beyond English.
- **Multilingualism and identity:** language contact, language choice, and identity in multilingual regions such as the border region around Trier.

## Workshops

Workshops offer a venue to discuss a focused research topic in depth, to bring together a specific community, or to explore emerging directions. Workshops can be held as **full-day** or **half-day** events. We currently plan full-day events for Sep. 06 (Mon) and half-day events for Sep. 07 (Tue), 2027; the final schedule will depend on the accepted proposals. Workshop organizers are responsible for their own call for papers, reviewing, and program. Accepted workshop papers will be due by the workshop camera-ready deadline listed under [Dates & Deadlines](/konvens-2027/dates).

Workshops may publish their proceedings as a volume of the KONVENS 2027 proceedings in the [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/). Organizers who choose this option agree to:

- apply scientific quality standards in their review process that are equivalent to those of the [ACL Rolling Review (ARR)](https://aclrollingreview.org/reviewerguidelines), while taking into account the scope of KONVENS as a venue, including at least two independent reviews per paper and a conflict-of-interest policy;
- provide their workshop proceedings as documents formatted according to the [ACLPUB guidelines](https://acl-org.github.io/ACLPUB/) (using the [ACL style files](https://github.com/acl-org/acl-style-files)), in line with the KONVENS 2027 deadlines.

Alternatively, workshops may remain non-archival, in which case accepted papers are not published in the ACL Anthology.

Workshop organizers who are members of the [German Society for Computational Linguistics and Language Technology (GSCL)](https://www.gscl.org/) can apply to the GSCL for funding, for example for the travel and accommodation of invited speakers. Applications must be submitted to the GSCL at least three months before the workshop, i.e., by early June 2027 for KONVENS 2027 workshops. Funding depends on the available budget and is decided by the GSCL board. Details and conditions are given in the [GSCL funding guidelines for workshops](https://www.gscl.org/activities/vergaberichtlinienworkshops).

**Workshop proposals should include:**

1. Title, short name or acronym, and a brief description of the topic, its relevance, and its relation to the KONVENS 2027 theme (if applicable).
2. Names, affiliations, short bios, and contact information of all organizers, with one designated contact person.
3. Preferred duration (full or half day) and planned format (e.g., paper presentations, posters, invited talks, panels, discussions).
4. Expected number of submissions and attendees, and a list of potential program committee members and invited speakers (if planned).
5. If the workshop has been held before: previous venues, number of submissions and accepted papers, and attendance.
6. Planned timeline for the workshop call for papers, reviewing, and notification, compatible with the KONVENS 2027 deadlines.
7. Whether the workshop proceedings will be published as a KONVENS 2027 volume in the ACL Anthology.
8. Plans for reaching a diverse audience and set of contributors.

## Tutorials

Tutorials offer an introduction to established or emerging methods, resources, or research areas for an audience of researchers and students. Tutorials can be held as **full-day** or **half-day** events, following the same schedule as workshops. Introductory and cutting-edge tutorials are both welcome, and either type may include hands-on components.

**Tutorial proposals should include:**

1. Title and a brief description of the tutorial content, its relevance, and its relation to the KONVENS 2027 theme (if applicable).
2. Type of tutorial (introductory or cutting-edge) and preferred duration (full or half day).
3. Outline of the tutorial structure with an estimated time allocation per part, and any hands-on components.
4. Target audience, expected prerequisites, and estimated number of participants.
5. Names, affiliations, short bios, and contact information of all presenters, including their expertise on the topic, with one designated contact person.
6. Technical requirements (e.g., participant laptops, software, data, computing resources).
7. Reading list and, if available, links to teaching materials.
8. Plans for reaching a diverse audience.

## Shared Tasks

Shared tasks offer a common evaluation framework for a well-defined research problem and foster comparable results across systems. We particularly welcome tasks involving German and the other languages and varieties of the Greater Region (Saarland, Lorraine, Luxembourg, Rhineland-Palatinate, and Wallonia), as well as tasks related to the conference theme. Shared task organizers are responsible for providing data, evaluation, and baselines, for running the task, for collecting and reviewing system description papers, and for presenting the results at KONVENS 2027 on Sep. 06 (Mon) or Sep. 07 (Tue), 2027.

Shared tasks may likewise publish their system description papers as a volume of the KONVENS 2027 proceedings in the [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/). Organizers who choose this option agree to the same review and formatting standards as workshops (see [Workshops](#workshops)). Alternatively, shared tasks may remain non-archival.

**Shared task proposals should include:**

1. Title, short name or acronym, and a description of the task, its motivation, and its relation to the KONVENS 2027 theme (if applicable).
2. Task definition, including subtasks (if any), input and output formats, and example instances (which may be provided in an appendix).
3. Description of the data: sources, size, languages or varieties, annotation procedure and quality, availability for training and testing, and licensing and ethical considerations.
4. Evaluation measures and planned baseline systems.
5. Expected number of participating teams and plans for advertising the task, including how to reach a diverse set of participants.
6. Planned timeline (data release, evaluation period, system description papers, reviewing), compatible with the KONVENS 2027 deadlines.
7. Preferred duration (full or half day) of the shared task session.
8. Whether the system description papers will be published as a KONVENS 2027 volume in the ACL Anthology.
9. Names, affiliations, short bios, and contact information of all organizers, with one designated contact person.

## Venue and Registration

All workshops, tutorials, and shared tasks are planned as in-person events at Trier University. KONVENS 2027 provides rooms, audiovisual equipment, and catering. Remote contributions are possible for invited speakers; other exceptions can be discussed with the organizing committee. All participants, including organizers, presenters, and attendees of workshops, tutorials, and shared tasks, are required to register. Details on registration, including options for attending satellite events only, will be announced on this page. All events and participants are expected to follow the [ACL Anti-Harassment Policy](https://www.aclweb.org/adminwiki/index.php/Anti-Harassment_Policy) and the [ACL Code of Ethics](https://www.aclweb.org/portal/content/acl-code-ethics).

## Evaluation of Proposals

Proposals will be evaluated by the KONVENS 2027 organizing committee based on their relevance to the computational linguistics community, scientific quality and clarity, expected interest and participation, feasibility of the plan and timeline, the qualifications of the organizers, and their relation to the conference theme. The committee may suggest merging proposals with closely related topics.

## Submission

Proposals should be submitted as a single PDF document of at most **2 pages**, formatted with the [ACL style files](https://github.com/acl-org/acl-style-files). References, organizer/presenter bios, lists of potential program committee members and invited speakers, and, for shared task proposals, an appendix with example instances do not count toward the page limit. Details on the submission procedure will be announced on this page.
