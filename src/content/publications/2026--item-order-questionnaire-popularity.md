Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed as synthetic survey respondents in psychological research, yet the conditions under which their responses achieve psychometric validity remain poorly understood. We investigate two factors hypothesized to systematically modulate LLM response quality on established questionnaires: item pre-sentation order and questionnaire popularity as an operationalization of likely training data coverage. We analyze whether grouping question-naire items by their measured construct, rather than cycling through constructs sequentially, improves internal consistency and cross-human structural alignment, and whether this effect is amplified for instruments more likely to be well-represented in LLM training corpora. We evaluate six open-source instruction-tuned LLMs spanning three model fami-lies on two instruments of markedly contrasting public prominence: the widely-known Big Five Personality Traits (Big 5) and the lesser-known Humor Styles Questionnaire (HSQ). Our results show that grouped item presentation consistently improves both metrics across all models and both instruments and instrument popularity reinforces this effect. Our findings indicate that LLM performance is partly driven by format-triggered pattern recognition within training data rather than generalizable construct understanding.
